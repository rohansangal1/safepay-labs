Our mission

Apps should be able to trust a payment message exactly as much as it deserves.

Payment companies move the money. Apps deliver the order. In between sits a plain text message that is easy to fake, repeat or alter. We exist to find out which designs make that moment safe, to measure it honestly, and to give those answers away.

  • Measure, don't assume

    We attack real code and record what the money and the orders actually did. No checklists, no self-reporting.

  • Count harm in money

    Underpaid orders, double deliveries, lost payments. We report outcomes a business would recognise, not abstract severity scores.

  • Give the answers away

    The study plan is published before the main run. The paper, the data and a free tool for checking your own checkout follow.

  • Fix first, publish second

    If we find a weakness in someone else's open-source software, its maintainers hear about it privately before anyone else.

The problem

An app gets a message saying “payment received.” What if the message is fake?

When you pay inside an app, the app usually never sees your card. The payment company takes the money, then sends the app a short message. The app delivers on the strength of it. If the app doesn't check carefully, a made-up, repeated or altered message costs real money.

Forged

A message the payment company never sent, dressed up to look real.

Replayed

A genuine message, captured once and delivered again.

Reordered

Real messages arriving in the wrong order, so a refund lands before the payment.

Mismatched

The right message attached to the wrong order, amount or account.

What it costs when a check is missing

The five outcomes we measure. Each one is money or product leaving the business that shouldn't.

  • Paid too littleAn order is marked paid for less than it cost.
  • Delivered twiceOne payment is counted again and the order is fulfilled twice.
  • Order lostThe customer paid, but the app never finds out.
  • Wrong accountA payment is credited to somebody else's order.
  • Refund mismatchMoney is refunded but the order still counts as paid.

Our approach

We build checkouts, attack them in our own lab, and measure what the money did.

  1. 01

    Build a careful reference

    A checkout we wrote ourselves that handles every case correctly, plus copies with one deliberate mistake each. They prove our tests can tell safe from unsafe.

  2. 02

    Attack it locally

    Our harness plays the attacker: forged, replayed, reordered and mismatched messages, delivered to software running on our own machines.

  3. 03

    Measure the harm

    We record what went wrong in money and orders, and give each checkout an assurance score that keeps anything we couldn't test visible rather than assuming it passed.

Where this is going

Beyond grading individual checkouts, we are mapping which architectures and security layers stop money-level harm outright, and how to guide the tools people increasingly build with, including AI coding assistants, toward producing those designs by default.

Who this is for

Safer payments help everyone on both sides of the checkout.

  • Apps and businesses

    Most apps rely on a plugin, an SDK or a few hundred lines of glue code. We find out which of those designs quietly leak money.

  • Customers

    Fewer lost orders, fewer “we never got your payment” emails, and less of your money stuck in dispute queues.

  • Developers

    A free checker for your own checkout and a clear list of the controls that matter most, in the order they matter.

How we keep it safe

Research that never touches a real app or payment account.

Only our own software

We test software we wrote or open-source software we run on our own machines. We never probe a live app or payment system we don't own.

No real money or card data

Every order, payment and customer in the lab is made up. Nothing is charged and nothing is delivered.

Fix first, publish second

Weaknesses in someone else's open-source software go to its maintainers privately before anything is published.

The assurance score is a research measure. It is not a PCI certification or a guarantee that a checkout is secure.
