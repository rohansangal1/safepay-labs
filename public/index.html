Our mission

Money should move exactly as intended: the right amount, to the right place, once. We find out which designs make that true, for the AI agents now handling payments and for the apps behind every checkout, measure it honestly, and give the answers away.

  • Measure, don't assume

    We run real code and real agents in our own sandbox and record what the money did.

  • Count harm in money

    Read from a ledger, in outcomes a business would recognise. No guesswork.

  • Give it away

    Methods, data and a free testing tool.

  • Fix first

    Maintainers hear privately before anyone else.

The problem

Two places money goes wrong.

When an AI agent holds the payment tools

Agents are starting to pay invoices, issue refunds and send payouts. We test what happens when the tools they depend on fail or mislead them.

01

A tool fails

A timeout mid-payment, and the retry pays twice.

02

A tool misleads

A planted tool description steers money to a payee the task never named.

03

A step goes too far

A refund or payout nobody asked for.

04

A report is wrong

The agent says it paid. The ledger says otherwise.

When an app trusts a payment message

Payment companies tell an app that a payment happened by sending it a message. There are four ways that message can lie.

01

Forged

A message the payment company never sent.

02

Replayed

A real message, delivered again.

03

Reordered

A refund lands before the payment.

04

Mismatched

Right message, wrong order, amount or account.

What we measure

When a safeguard is missing, it costs money.

Our approach

Build it. Attack it. Measure the money.

  1. 01

    Build a sandbox we own

    A payments service with a strict double-entry ledger and a careful reference checkout, plus copies with one deliberate flaw each.

  2. 02

    Stress it locally

    Agents get payment tools that fail, time out or are described misleadingly. Checkouts get forged, replayed, reordered and mismatched messages. Every run starts in a fresh, sealed environment on our own machines.

  3. 03

    Measure the harm

    We compare the ledger with what should have happened, so duplicate, misrouted and lost money is counted, not guessed. Checkouts also get a score that never counts an untested check as a pass.

  4. 04

    Compare the designs

    Idempotency keys, payee and amount rules, confirm-before-send, pinned tool lists: which ones actually prevent the harm.
